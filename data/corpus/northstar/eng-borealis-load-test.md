# Project Borealis: Load Test Results

This fictional report is for members of Project Borealis only. It records the load tests of the usage-based pricing engine before the second internal milestone.

## Test setup

The tests ran against a copy of the pricing engine in the staging region with three instances, each with four processor cores. Usage events were generated by a replay tool from one week of anonymized metering data, scaled up to the target rate. The load profile was:

```text
phase      duration   events per second
warm-up    10 min     2,000
ramp       20 min     2,000 -> 12,000
steady     60 min     12,000
spike      5 min      30,000
recovery   15 min     12,000
```

## Results

| Phase | Median latency | 99th percentile latency | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| steady | 18 ms | 95 ms | 0.00 percent |
| spike | 41 ms | 1,850 ms | 0.40 percent |
| recovery | 19 ms | 110 ms | 0.00 percent |

During the spike phase the queue of unpriced events grew to about 900,000 events and was drained within seven minutes after the spike ended. Every error during the spike was a timeout of the rating call; no event was lost or priced twice.

## Conclusions

Three instances carry the steady target with room to spare. The spike latency is above the goal of one second at the 99th percentile, so the team will test five instances before the next milestone. The rating call will also get a separate connection pool, because the timeouts came from rating calls waiting behind slow writes of priced events.
